# Manifesto: Pass the Baton

## Core message

**What applies to the people should apply to those who lead them.**

All over the world, people hand their work over to the next generation at a certain age. We ask leaders to do the same. Not out of distrust of older people, but out of respect for renewal.

## Why: the problem

Around the world, many leaders are far past the age at which their citizens retire. This holds in democracies and in authoritarian regimes alike.

- **A double standard.** A nurse, a builder or a teacher hands over their work. Those who decide over millions of lives often stay until the very end.
- **A short time horizon.** Decisions on climate, debt and pensions mostly affect generations who will bear the consequences themselves.
- **Renewal is blocked.** Those who never leave make no room for new ideas, new people and new energy.
- **Too little attention for the role.** World leadership demands full commitment, every day. That is a heavy task at any age, and all the more so at an advanced age.

## The demand

**Anyone who has reached the statutory retirement age of their own country, and at the latest 67, does not start a new term in one of the highest offices.**

- Applies worldwide, in every country and under every regime, starting with the highest offices: head of state, head of government and ministers. In a second phase we extend it to other political offices.
- Ties the limit to what the people already accept: the retirement age, with a worldwide ceiling of 67.
- A term already under way may be completed. A new one is not started.
- Ordinary citizens remain free to keep working. Those who lead a country carry a heavier responsibility and fall under a stricter norm, like pilots and judges.

## Our principles

1. **Equality.** The same age norm for leaders as for the citizens they serve.
2. **Respect.** We are not against older people. Experience remains valuable, as adviser, mentor or ambassador.
3. **Renewal.** Passing the baton is an act of leadership, not a defeat.
4. **Non-violence.** We persuade; we never use force.
5. **Independence.** We take no side, left or right. The norm applies to everyone.

## Answers to objections

| Objection | Our answer |
| --- | --- |
| Age says nothing about the individual. | True, but we already accept a fixed limit for judges and pilots. A clear rule is fairer than debating every time who is still "fit enough". |
| This is age discrimination. | Everyone keeps the right to keep working. Only the highest office carries a stricter norm, because it affects millions of lives. |
| Voters must be free to choose. | Voters already accept limits, such as term limits and minimum ages. A maximum age is their logical counterpart. |
| Experience matters most in a crisis. | That experience doesn't disappear. Former leaders can advise, mediate and inspire without holding power. |
| In dictatorships the people have no voice. | That is why this must become a worldwide norm. The more self-evident it is elsewhere, the greater the pressure on those who evade it. |

## How we do it: symbol, moment, action

- **Symbol: the baton.** A relay baton, as image, gesture or object. People pass it to each other online and in the street. Recognisable in every language and culture.
- **Hashtag:** #PassTheBaton, with local variants such as #StokjeDoorgeven.
- **Moment: one worldwide action day a year.** On the same day, people everywhere symbolically pass the baton, in squares, at work and online.
- **Faces.** Young people as well as retirees who have already passed it on. This shows it is not young against old.
- **Local translation.** Each country ties the demand to its own retirement age and its own leaders.
- **First steps.** Start as soon as possible, in two phases: first online (manifesto, website with sign-up, hashtag), then a first physical action day once there are supporters in several countries.

## Possible figureheads

A strong figurehead embodies the message. Profiles to approach:

| Profile | Why it works |
| --- | --- |
| A former leader who stepped down voluntarily and early | Living proof that stepping down is an act of leadership. The strongest credibility. |
| A retiree who passed on the baton themselves | Proves this is not "young against old". Speaks to older voters. |
| A relay athlete | The baton is literally their craft. Well known, likeable and politically neutral. |
| A young activist or student | Brings energy and reach among young people and on social media. |
| A duo: one older, one younger | The image of passing it on itself. Possibly the strongest choice. |
