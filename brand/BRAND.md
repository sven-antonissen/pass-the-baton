# Pass the Baton: Brand Guide

## Why

**We exist so that those who lead a country hand over at the same age as the people they serve.**

Around the world, people hand their work to the next generation at retirement age, while many who decide over millions of lives stay on. Pass the Baton asks that anyone who has reached the statutory retirement age of their own country, and at the latest 67, does not start a new term in one of the highest offices. We say it out of respect for renewal, not out of distrust of older people. Experience stays valuable, as adviser, mentor or ambassador. We are non-violent, non-partisan and global. Passing the baton is an act of leadership, not a defeat.

## Tone of voice

We sound **calm, warm, clear and firm**. We invite, we do not accuse. We talk like a good relay team: encouraging, direct, together.

### Do's

1. **Speak about handing over, not about age as a flaw.**
   *"Handing over is an act of leadership."*
2. **Use plain, short sentences that translate easily.**
   *"The same rule for those who lead as for those they serve."*
3. **Show both generations together.**
   *"Retirees who have passed it on and students who are ready to carry it: one team."*

### Don'ts

1. **Don't mock, label or stereotype older people.**
   *Not: "Time for the old guard to go."* Instead: *"Experience stays. Power moves on."*
2. **Don't take sides, name parties or attack individuals.**
   *Not: "Party X's leader is too old."* Instead: *"The norm applies to everyone, left or right."*
3. **Don't use war, violence or aggressive slogans.**
   *Not: "Fight the old regime."* Instead: *"We persuade. Join us and pass the baton."*

## Taglines

1. **Pass the baton.** (strongest: short, a call to action, works as hashtag #PassTheBaton)
2. **Lead. Then hand over.**
3. **Room for what comes next.**
4. **Renewal is a relay.**
5. **The same rule for those who lead.**

Local versions are welcome, such as #StokjeDoorgeven. Translate the idea, not word for word, and keep the baton.

## Colour palette

The palette is deliberately not tied to any party, country or religion. It pairs a deep neutral with a cool and a warm accent that differ strongly in lightness, so they stay distinguishable in black and white and for colour-blind viewers.

| Name | Hex | Use |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Relay Ink | `#1B2230` | Main text, dark backgrounds, outlines in logos |
| Warm Paper | `#FBF7F0` | Main background, text on dark colours |
| Track Teal | `#0B6E6E` | Headings accents, links, buttons, the "giving" side |
| Handover Amber | `#F0A202` | The baton, highlights, calls to action (fill only) |
| Quiet Stone | `#5B6472` | Secondary text, captions, borders |

| Warm Paper on Relay Ink | about 14.8 : 1 | AAA |
| Track Teal on Warm Paper | about 5.7 : 1 | AA |

**Rule:** Handover Amber is never used for text on Warm Paper (about 2 : 1). On light backgrounds use amber only as a fill with an Relay Ink outline or Relay Ink text on top. Never rely on colour alone to carry meaning; pair it with shape, label or position.

## Typography

Both typefaces are free (SIL Open Font License) and available on Google Fonts.

- **Headings: Lexend**, Bold (700). Designed for easy reading, with wide, open letterforms.
- **Body: Atkinson Hyperlegible**, Regular (400) and Bold (700). Designed for maximum character distinction and legibility.
- **Other scripts:** use the matching **Noto Sans** family (for example Noto Sans Arabic, Noto Sans Devanagari, Noto Sans SC) for headings and body, so every language is fully supported.

| Element | Size (desktop / mobile) | Line height |
| --- | --- | --- |
| H1 | 48 px / 36 px | 1.15 |
| H2 | 36 px / 28 px | 1.2 |
| H3 | 24 px / 22 px | 1.3 |
| Body | 18 px / 17 px (never below 16 px) | 1.6 |
| Caption | 14 px | 1.5 |

Left-align text (right-align for right-to-left scripts). Keep lines to about 60-75 characters.

## Logo

All three logos are original constructions from simple geometric shapes. They are built around one idea: **a baton changing hands**. Each works in colour, in one colour and in black and white. Keep clear space around the logo equal to the width of the baton. Minimum size for symbols: 24 px on screen, 10 mm in print. The lockup with text (logo 2) should not go below 120 px wide; use a symbol at smaller sizes.

### Logo 1: "The Handover" (`logo-1.svg`)

Two batons side by side, offset and overlapping on a diagonal: one paper-coloured, one amber, separated by a dark gap. It shows the moment of passing, from one runner to the next, and the diagonal gives it upward movement. **Use it as** the main symbol: social avatars, favicon, stickers, badges, app icon and anything small or square.

### Logo 2: "The Duo" (`logo-2.svg`)

A larger teal circle and a smaller amber circle, joined by one baton passing through both. They stand for two people, one older and one younger, in the act of passing it on, with no hierarchy between them. Next to it sits the wordmark "Pass the Baton". **Use it as** the full signature: website header, letters, petitions, press material and banners. The wordmark is live text set in Lexend Bold (falls back to a generic sans-serif if Lexend is not installed). For print production, convert the text to outlines with the font installed.

### Logo 3: "The Open Ring" (`logo-3.svg`)

A teal ring, like a running track or a closed circle of power, with an opening through which an amber baton leaves. The message: the circle opens and something moves on. **Use it as** a seal for posters, action-day material, flags, t-shirts and large formats, where a bold circular mark works well.

**Which to choose:** small or square, use logo 1. Horizontal and with the name, use logo 2. Big, graphic and symbolic, use logo 3. Do not combine them, recolour them outside the palette, stretch them or place them on busy photos without a solid background.

## The baton

The baton is our one symbol. It is a simple rounded bar, always shown **in the act of being passed**, never held up as a trophy or used as a weapon. It is recognisable in every language and culture. Amber is its signature colour.

- **Online:** a diagonal amber baton with a dark outline is our sticker, emoji-style icon and profile frame. People post a photo or illustration of themselves passing a baton (or a similar object) with **#PassTheBaton** or a local variant. In avatars, use logo 1.
- **On posters:** one large baton on a Warm Paper or Relay Ink background, running diagonally off the edge to suggest it travels on. A short line of text in Lexend Bold, such as "Pass the baton." Keep a lot of white space. Always show people from different generations together, never one against the other.
- **As a physical object:** a simple, safe, lightweight tube, amber with a Relay Ink band at each end, so it is visible and works in black-and-white photos. People pass it to each other in squares, at work and at events on the yearly worldwide action day. Add the hashtag and the website, and nothing more. Never use it to block, push or threaten anyone.
- **With athletes:** relay athletes can carry or pass the baton at sports events, runs and relays, as a neutral and friendly gesture. Use our own baton design, never any federation's, event's or sponsor's marks, and never the Olympic rings or anything resembling them. Athletes take part as individuals and do not speak for their federation or country unless they say so themselves.
- **Gesture:** two hands, one giving and one receiving. The older and the younger hand can be either one. Show it with different skin tones, ages and abilities.

## Accessibility and neutrality checklist

- Works in black and white: dark and light shapes differ in lightness, not only in hue.
- Colour-blind safe: teal and amber differ strongly in lightness, and meaning is never carried by colour alone.
- Any script: logos 1 and 3 contain no text; logo 2's text can be replaced by a local wordmark in the same typeface family.
- Neutral: no national colours, flags, party colours or religious symbols.
- Respectful of age: images show handing over as shared and positive, never age as a joke.
