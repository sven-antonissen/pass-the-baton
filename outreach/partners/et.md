# Partner candidates: Ethiopia (ET)

Status: **no outreach recommended at this time. No organisations listed and no e-mail drafted. Nothing has been sent.**
Research date: 2026-10-19.

## Why we are not proposing outreach

Activism carries real risk in Ethiopia right now. Public sources found in this run point the same way:

- A joint statement by FIDH and OMCT (the Observatory for the Protection of Human Rights Defenders, 28 May 2026) voices serious concern about civic space and the safety of human rights defenders around the 1 June 2026 election. It reports journalist arrests and a revoked press accreditation. https://www.fidh.org/en/region/Africa/ethiopia/ethiopia-civic-space-and-security-at-risk-ahead-of-the-7th-general
- The same statement says a draft CSO law would put governance under state control, ban international advocacy funding and let the Ministry of Justice dissolve organisations. As of that statement it was pending and not yet in parliament. About 1,500 CSOs were dissolved in 2024 for administrative non-compliance.
- ICNL's Civic Freedom Monitor says the constitution guarantees expression and assembly, but in practice these rights are undermined by restrictive laws, arbitrary enforcement and excessive force against protesters. https://www.icnl.org/resources/civic-freedom-monitor/ethiopia
- The CIVICUS Monitor rated Ethiopia's civic space "closed" in December 2024, as reported by Policy Magazine. https://www.policymagazine.ca/voices-in-the-shadows-how-ethiopias-shrinking-civic-space-silences-its-people/
- In December 2024 the regulator suspended two prominent human rights organisations. Suspensions were later lifted, per FIDH.

On the other side, Ethiopian state-linked media describe expanding civic space and youth participation before the 2026 election. This is a government-friendly view and is not reflected in independent monitors.

A message that talks about when national leaders should step down is politically sensitive in this setting. An unsolicited e-mail could expose a local organisation to scrutiny, or be read as foreign political interference. That could harm the organisation, and it conflicts with our principle of doing no harm.

## Suggested approach instead

1. **Do not contact organisations inside Ethiopia for now.** No local organisations are listed here on purpose. A public list of "possible supporters" could itself put them at risk.
2. **If a maintainer wants to engage, start with the diaspora and international level.** Consider only organisations that are based outside Ethiopia, that already work publicly on the subject, and that are asked what they consider safe. This needs a separate, careful look.
3. **Let local interest come to us.** If someone in Ethiopia contacts the movement, reply only through the channel they chose. Let them set the pace. Do not publish names, and do not ask for personal data.
4. **Keep the global message neutral.** Our manifesto applies to every country under every regime. Avoid naming Ethiopian leaders or parties in any material aimed at Ethiopia.
5. **Re-check the situation before any change.** Look at the civic-space ratings and the status of the draft CSO law. The picture may change.
