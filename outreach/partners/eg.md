# Partner candidates: Egypt (EG)

Status: **Outreach not recommended. No organisations are listed and no draft e-mail has been written.**
Research date: 2026-10-18. Nothing has been sent.

## Why we recommend caution instead of outreach

Activism and independent civil society carry real risk in Egypt. Sources checked in this run:

- **Human Rights Watch, World Report 2026 (Egypt):** civic space remains severely curtailed, peaceful protests and gatherings are effectively banned, and authorities punish dissenting expression. https://www.hrw.org/world-report/2026/country-chapters/egypt
- **Amnesty International (Nov 2025 briefing on NGOs in Egypt):** the 2019 associations law and security-agency interference create a climate of intimidation and surveillance for independent NGOs. https://www.amnesty.org/en/latest/news/2025/11/egypt-reverse-sweeping-controls-over-independent-civil-society-organizations/
- **ICNL Civic Freedom Monitor (Egypt):** Law 149 of 2019 makes it very difficult for independent organisations to operate, and working with unauthorised organisations can bring heavy fines. https://www.icnl.org/resources/civic-freedom-monitor/egypt
- **BTI 2026 country report (Egypt):** many critical organisations have been shut down, and small protests by activists, students and journalists are repressed, often with arrests. https://bti-project.org/en/reports/country-report/EGY

Our demand concerns who may start a new term in the highest offices. An association with it could be read as political, and even a polite first e-mail could expose an organisation or its staff to scrutiny. Being named in an outreach list or a shared message could also create risk for them.

## Suggested approach

1. **No cold outreach** to organisations inside Egypt. Do not send the movement's material to Egyptian youth, student or civic groups.
2. **Do not name or list** Egyptian organisations or individuals as partners or supporters, now or in public materials.
3. If the maintainers want a presence, consider **international or diaspora routes** only, such as international youth or democracy networks that can speak for themselves and choose their own level of visibility. Talk to people with regional and digital-security expertise first.
4. If anyone in Egypt contacts us on their own initiative, reply carefully and let them lead on what is safe. Do not publish their details.
5. Revisit this decision if the situation changes. The sources above are the basis for it.
