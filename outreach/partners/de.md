## Activism risk

None beyond normal civic risk. Germany has a free civil society. The one practical point: many German non-profits are careful about political positions because of charitable-status (Gemeinnützigkeit) rules, so expect some to decline politely.

## Before contacting: maintainer checks

- **Fill in the placeholders** and use only the general routes above. Do not use the staff names that appear on some regional pages (for example Mehr Demokratie's regional offices). I did not copy them.
- **DBJR:** the site `dbjr.de` is taken from the Bundestag lobby register and the federal directory, not opened directly. Open it, confirm it is current and find the contact page. Its members are mostly youth branches of churches, unions and other organisations. Some of them are tied to parties or religious bodies, so be careful if you ever approach members.
- **SRzG:** open the site and confirm it is active and find a contact page. Its German Wikipedia entry notes criticism that "generational justice" framing can be read as targeting older people's benefits (pensions). The movement says it is not against older people, so decide whether the fit holds and keep the tone clear.
- **Generationen Stiftung:** the pages found in this run are partly several years old (campaigns up to 2021). Confirm it is still active before writing. It has said that actions risking its charitable status are limited, so it may be cautious about taking new positions.
- **Mehr Demokratie:** its focus is direct democracy, so an age limit for office-holders is a stretch beyond its core topic. Say so honestly in the message.
- Check the retirement age in `data/countries/de.json` before putting any number into a message. I left it out on purpose.
- None of these organisations has been shown to support the movement. The drafts say so.
