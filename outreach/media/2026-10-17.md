## 1. Most of the world lives under a leader past retirement age (analysis)

- **Headline (our words):** New data analysis shows that most people worldwide live in a country led by someone older than 65.
- **Outlet:** Hannah Ritchie's Substack newsletter (an analysis, not a news report; please verify the author's credentials and exact publication date before use)
- **Date:** around 15 October 2026 (the search listing said "2 days ago")
- **Link:** https://hannahritchie.substack.com/p/how-old-are-leaders
- **Key facts in the piece:** The article's title says one in ten people are older than 65, yet three in four live under a leader who is. It puts the median age of leaders worldwide at around 64, with Africa the oldest region at nearly 70.
- **Why it matters:** It puts numbers on the double standard at the heart of our manifesto: leaders are often well beyond the age at which their citizens hand over their own work. It is a good, calm, fact-based hook that blames no one.
- **Draft social post (under 280 characters):**
  > Most of the world lives under a leader who is past the age when most people retire. Nurses, builders and teachers hand over their work. Handing over at the top is not a defeat, it is leadership. #PassTheBaton

- **Headline (our words):** A writer re-runs US Supreme Court history to test what staggered 18-year terms and a retirement age of 75 would have changed.
- **Outlet:** Jacob Candelaria's Substack (a personal opinion and data piece, not a news report; please treat it as commentary)
- **Date:** 16 October 2026
- **Link:** https://jacobcandelaria.substack.com/p/the-accidental-term-limit
- **Key facts in the piece:** The author re-ran the Court's history as if the Constitution had set staggered eighteen-year terms from the start, and also looks at a retirement age of 75.
- **Why it matters:** It shows that even in a system built on lifetime tenure, people are asking for a fixed, predictable handover. It is about judges, not the highest political offices, so use it as an illustration of the principle only. The manifesto already says we accept fixed limits for judges and pilots.
- **Draft social post (under 280 characters):**

## Op-ed angle

**Title:** *A Clear Rule Is a Kind Rule: Why Handing Over Is an Act of Leadership*

- **Open with the numbers.** An analysis published this week finds that three in four people live under a leader older than 65, while only about one in ten people is that old (Ritchie, see story 1). Use it as a mirror, not an accusation. Our concern is the double standard, not older people.
- **Rules protect people.** Without a clear age norm, we end up arguing about whether individuals are "still fit enough", which is unfair and painful for everyone. A fixed rule spares leaders and voters that debate. Link to the manifesto's point about judges and pilots, and to this week's Supreme Court commentary (story 3).
- **Limits are already normal.** Countries such as Malaysia are debating term limits right now (story 2). A maximum age for starting a new term, tied to each country's own retirement age and capped at 67, is the logical counterpart to term limits and minimum ages.
- **Experience does not disappear.** Former leaders can advise, mediate and mentor without holding power. Passing the baton is an act of leadership, and the best figureheads are those who did it voluntarily and early.
- **Close with the invitation.** The rule applies equally to every party and every country, with no one singled out. Invite readers to join the worldwide action day and pass the baton in their own workplace, square or online. #PassTheBaton
