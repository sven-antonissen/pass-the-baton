## Story 1: US House Democrats weigh term limits for committee leaders

- **Headline (own words):** Democrats may take the House majority with long-serving faces in key roles, and some members are discussing term limits for committee leaders.
- **Outlet:** Associated Press (by Kevin Freking), read via The Washington Times; also carried by WSLS
- **Date:** 9 October 2026
- **Link:** https://washingtontimes.com/news/2026/oct/9/time-term-limits-democrats-majority-could-familiar-faces-key-roles (also https://www.wsls.com/news/politics/2026/10/09/democrats-eye-return-to-power-with-many-of-the-same-faces-crafting-their-agenda/)
- **What the article reports (paraphrased):**
  - Many of the same people who have steered the party's legislative work for a decade or more would likely lead if the party wins the majority.
  - The idea of limits is being discussed behind the scenes.
  - In 2022, Democrats voted overwhelmingly against a six-year limit for committee leaders.
  - At least two members, Reps. Vindman and Houlahan, are quoted as open to the idea. Houlahan says she is in the minority on it.
  - Party leaders are playing the issue down for now.
- **Why it matters to the movement:** It shows that renewal inside institutions is being debated openly, with arguments for it from within the party. It is about committee roles, not the highest offices, so we should tie it to our principle and not claim it as our cause.
- **Draft social post (≈250 characters):**

## Op-ed angle

**Title:** A Clear Rule Is Kinder Than a Verdict on Every Person

- **Open with the pattern.** In the US this week, parties are discussing limits on committee leaders, and primary voters often backed older incumbents. Both stories are about how renewal happens, and neither is about any one person.
- **Name the incentive problem, not the individuals.** An America Magazine editorial from August 2026 argued that seniority and incumbency advantages keep long-serving people running. It said this calls for a structural answer. This source is older than seven days, so check it before use.
- **Offer the principle.** The highest offices should follow the same retirement-age norm that nurses, builders and teachers already follow, with a worldwide ceiling of 67. A norm that applies to everyone spares us from judging individuals one by one.
- **Answer "let voters decide".** Voters already accept limits such as term limits and minimum ages. A maximum age at the start of a new term is the logical counterpart. A term already under way can be completed.
- **Close warmly.** Experience does not vanish. Former leaders can advise, mediate and mentor. Passing the baton is an act of leadership, and it is the same in every party and every country.
