## Story 1: Young candidates are running for Congress in record numbers

- **Headline (own words):** Generational change is coming to Congress, but it won't fix everything
- **Outlet:** The Hill (opinion piece by the head of Future Caucus, a cross-party group for millennial and Gen Z lawmakers)
- **Date:** about 22 Oct 2026 (listed as two days old when searched; the piece says the US midterm vote is less than two weeks away)
- **Link:** https://thehill.com/opinion/campaign/6163225-millennials-gen-z-congress-turnover/
- **What it reports:** For the first time, more than a third of people who filed to run for Congress are millennials or Gen Z, up from about one in six in 2022. It also says roughly eight in ten people of all parties support age limits for members. Six of the 11 incumbents who lost primaries this year lost to younger challengers. The author also points out that younger candidates are less likely to reach the general-election ballot.
- **Why it matters to us:** It shows broad, cross-party appetite for renewal, and it is honest that age alone doesn't fix politics, which fits our line that this is about handing over, not about older people. Congress is not one of the "highest offices" in phase one of our demand, so frame this as the public mood, not as our exact ask.
- **Caveats:** This is an opinion piece by an advocate for younger lawmakers. Treat its figures as the author's claims, and check the underlying Future Caucus analysis before quoting numbers. Keep our message non-partisan: this is about midterm candidates of every party, and we should not back any candidate.
- **Draft social post (≈235 characters):**
  > Roughly 8 in 10 Americans, across parties, back age limits for Congress, says a new op-ed. We're not against experience. We're for renewal. A clear rule beats arguing about individuals. #PassTheBaton

## Story 2 (borderline, one day outside the window): A way around the presidential term limit is being floated in Turkey

- **Headline (own words):** Turkish officials hint at a legal route for the president to run again beyond the two-term limit
- **Outlet:** Algemeiner
- **Date:** 15 Oct 2026 (about 8–9 days old, so just outside the seven-day window; include only if the maintainer agrees)
- **Link:** https://www.algemeiner.com/2026/10/15/erdogan-expected-bypass-term-limits-run-president-again-crackdown-opposition/
- **What it reports:** Turkey's constitution limits the president to two terms. According to the report, officials close to the government say an election will be held in 2028 to re-elect the president. Under the constitution, parliament can call early elections, which could let a president in a second term run again, and that takes 360 of the 600 votes. Elections are currently scheduled for May 2028.
- **Why it matters to us:** It is a live example of a limit on time in office being open to workarounds. That supports our case for a simple, clear norm that applies the same way to everyone, though ours is an age norm and not a term limit.
- **Caveats:** A single source, and the outlet has its own editorial perspective. The article is built on statements by officials, not on a decision. Confirm with a wire service (Reuters, AP, AFP) before anything goes out. Do not comment on the individual, his age or his health. The post below talks only about the principle.
- **Draft social post (≈215 characters):**
  > Limits on time in power only work if they can't be sidestepped. Our idea is simple and global: one clear norm for every leader in every country, so handing over is the rule, not the exception. #PassTheBaton

## Op-ed angle

**Title:** A Clear Rule Is Kinder Than a Debate About Who Is "Still Fit"

- **Open with the mood.** A new cross-party op-ed says about eight in ten Americans support age limits for Congress, while a record share of candidates are millennials or Gen Z. People are asking for renewal, and it isn't a left–right issue. (Source: The Hill, see Story 1; verify the figures first.)
- **Move the debate from the person to the rule.** Arguing about whether one leader is "still fit" is unfair to that person and to everyone watching. A rule that covers all leaders in the same way takes the personal question off the table. We never need to make judgements about anyone's health.
- **Use familiar comparisons.** Judges and pilots already work under fixed limits, and nobody calls that an insult. Leaders of countries carry at least as much responsibility, so the same logic applies. Anyone is free to keep working. The stricter norm applies only to the very highest offices.
- **Rules that bend lose trust.** The reports from Turkey about getting around a term limit show why a norm must be clear, simple and the same for everyone. Keep this about the design of the rule, and do not make it about any one person. Add a second example from a different region once one is verified.
- **Close warmly: passing the baton is leadership.** Experience doesn't vanish. Former leaders can advise, mediate and mentor. Hold up the image of the relay: a duo, one older and one younger, handing over in mid-stride. A term in progress is completed, and a new one is simply not started.
