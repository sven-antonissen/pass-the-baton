Passing it on is leadership.

All over the world, people hand their work to the next generation at a certain age. We ask leaders to do the same. Not out of distrust of older people, but out of respect for renewal.

Read the manifesto Help build it

The demand

Anyone who has reached the statutory retirement age of their own country, and at the latest 67, does not start a new term in one of the highest offices.

It applies worldwide, in every country and under every regime, starting with heads of state, heads of government and ministers. A term already under way may be completed. Ordinary citizens remain free to keep working.

See how countries compare, with sources for every fact.

Why

A double standard

A nurse, a builder or a teacher hands over their work. Those who decide over millions of lives often stay until the very end.

A short horizon

Decisions on climate, debt and pensions mostly affect generations who will bear the consequences themselves.

Renewal is blocked

Those who never leave make no room for new ideas, new people and new energy.

Respect for experience

Experience stays valuable, as adviser, mentor or ambassador. Passing the baton is an act of leadership, not a defeat.
